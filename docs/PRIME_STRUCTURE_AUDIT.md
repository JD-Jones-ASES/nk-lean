# Prime structure review

This note records an AI-assisted review of the arithmetic and capacity proofs,
together with their exact controls. The accepted official Comparator replay is
documented separately in [VERIFICATION.md](VERIFICATION.md).

## Unit-power stabilization

[UnitConductor.lean](../NK/UnitConductor.lean) defines the sufficient conductor
`s(p,k)` as `v_p(k) + 1` for odd primes, `1` for `p = 2` and odd
`k`, and `v_2(k) + 2` for `p = 2` and even `k`.
For every prime `p`, `k ≥ 1`, unit input `p ∤ u`, and `n ≥ s(p,k)`,
`unitPower_stabilizes` proves

```text
(∃ z : Nat, z^k % p^n = u % p^n)
  ↔ (∃ z : Nat, z^k % p^s = u % p^s).
```

This includes singular odd primes and every even binary exponent. For odd
primes, the proof compares power-image indices in cyclic unit groups. For
`p = 2`, the kernel of unit reduction modulo 4 is cyclic of order
`2^(n−2)`, generated by 5; even-power roots are normalized by changing their
sign.

Under the same prime and positive-exponent hypotheses,
`unitConductor_le_exponent_iff` proves `s(p,k) ≤ k` exactly when
`(p,k) ≠ (2,2)`. Thus `unitPower_stabilizes_block` gives stabilization at
`p^k` outside that exception. The counterexample is 5, which is a square
modulo 4 but not modulo 16. The conductor is sufficient; universal minimality
is not claimed.

## Block graph and capacity

[PrimeBlockLex.lean](../NK/PrimeBlockLex.lean) proves that the full power graph
modulo `(p^k)^e` is the first-difference graph on `e` base-`p^k` blocks,
read from low to high, for prime `p`, `k,e ≥ 1`, and `(p,k) ≠ (2,2)`.
The reverse implication handles nonunit differences by removing common lower
blocks and lifting the residual unit. The digit equivalence
`blockWordEquiv b e hb : Fin (b^e) ≃ (Fin e → Fin b)` includes leading zeros
and the empty word at `e = 0`. The arc theorem itself assumes positive depth.
For binary squares, the block graph admits the pair `0 → 5` at depth two,
whereas the full graph modulo 16 rejects it.

[PrimeBlockCapacity.lean](../NK/PrimeBlockCapacity.lean) defines ordinary
interval capacity as the supremum over all selected supports and admissible
intervals in the full modular power graph. It proves

```text
modularCapacity k (p^(k*e)) f = (modularCapacity k (p^k) f)^e
```

for prime `p`, `k,e ≥ 1`, `f ≥ 0`, and `(p,k) ≠ (2,2)`. The proof
uses the graph equivalence and the ordinary lexicographic capacity theorem;
it does not assume a capacity recurrence. The conversion from
`IntervalAlphabet` preserves its moment exactly, so the capacity bound also
applies directly to every such alphabet.

This is a barrier within ordinary interval capacity at full block depths.
It is not an upper bound for the integer extremal problem. It makes no claim
for partial block depths, coupled distinct primes, negative `f`, binary
squares, or the numerical optimum of the one-block capacity.

## Controls

The two control files contain 24 positive and negative examples and 15 axiom
reports. They cover digit orientation, singular lifting, nonunit differences,
insufficient conductor precision, the unit-input hypothesis, and capacity
specializations for squares, singular cubes, binary fourth powers, and
`k = 1`. The axiom reports contain only `propext`, `Classical.choice`,
and `Quot.sound`.

From the repository root:

```sh
lake build NK.UnitConductor NK.PrimeBlockCapacity
lake env lean audit/unit-conductor-audit.lean
lake env lean audit/prime-block-audit.lean
```

These source reviews and controls supplement the accepted replay; they do not
constitute human peer review.
